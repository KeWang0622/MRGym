# Volume worlds: the third dimension of the hidden object

Generated by `python -m mrgym.scanner.volume_check --write` (engine `0.3.0`, NumPy 2.2.6, x86_64, one thread) from `docs/physics/volume.json`. Do not edit: regenerate. Times and memory are those of the machine of that run.

A 2D world is one axial section. The world kinds `head3d` and `qa3d` are objects with structure along the third axis (`src/mrgym/scanner/volume.py`). This page says what they are and how they are evaluated. **No scan is formed of a volume world yet**: a volume world has no fields, and the engine images 2D worlds only. Fields in 3D and the slice engine are the next packages.

## 1. Coordinates

Patient coordinates in mm from the isocentre. A vector is written (y, x, z), the order of the engine's arrays: +y is posterior, +x is the patient's left, +z is superior (towards the top of the head). B0 is along +z. (x, y, z) is the right-handed DICOM patient system (left, posterior, superior).

```text
            +z superior (head)                   axial image (row +y, col +x)
             |                                         anterior
             |                                   +----------------+
             |                                   |                |
             o--------- +x  patient's left     R |    (y, x)      | L     row index grows downwards (+y)
            /                                    |                |       column index grows to the right (+x)
           /                                     +----------------+
         +y posterior                                 posterior
```

A plane is a `Frame`: an origin and three orthonormal vectors `row`, `col`, `normal`. Grid point (i, j) of an n x n grid of extent E lies at origin + (i - n // 2) (E / n) row + (j - n // 2) (E / n) col. That is the engine's in-plane rule, so the axial frame through the isocentre is the grid of every 2D world.

| Standard plane | row (down on the image) | col (to the right) | normal |
|---|---|---|---|
| axial | +y: anterior at the top | +x: the patient's left on the right | +z |
| coronal | -z: the top of the head at the top | +x | +y |
| sagittal | -z | +y: anterior on the left | +x |

The normal of a standard plane is the positive patient axis, so a position along it is the patient coordinate. `rot_deg` turns a frame in its plane as `world.turned` does; `tilt_deg` tilts it about its row axis, then about its column axis. A lattice is an array [z, y, x]; its origin and spacing are given per array axis.

## 2. What the two recipes hold

A world is a recipe: shapes with a signed distance (ellipsoids, tubes, planes, layers of a depth below a surface), drawn from the seed. Nothing is stored as voxels. Boundaries are soft with the half-width of the 2D worlds (0.5 mm), so a point near a boundary holds two classes whose weights sum to 1.

**`head3d`** (11 classes with the lesions: skin, scalp_fat, skull, diploe, csf, wm, gm, deep_gm, muscle, blood, lesion_ms). An egg-shaped head behind a smooth warp, narrowing below its centre into a neck that goes on downwards out of the imaged cube. From outside in: skin, scalp fat with a varying thickness, then either the cranial cavity or its shell. The shell is skull (two tables with fatty marrow between them) within the bone thickness of the cavity and muscle elsewhere (face and neck), with two eyes (drawn with the CSF values) and air: a frontal and a sphenoid sinus, two mastoid regions and an airway. The cavity holds CSF and the brain: cortex and white matter, sulci as the zero sheets of a smooth random field limited in depth, an interhemispheric fissure interrupted by a callosal region, three ellipsoids per lateral ventricle and a third ventricle, three deep nuclei per side. Options: lesions (`lesion="ms"`: seeded at points the recipe itself reports as white matter, with a hidden sign and size of their susceptibility), straight vessels from the neck into the brain, each with a hidden direction and a hidden signed speed (`volume=dict(vessels=n)`), placed inserts (`volume=dict(inserts=[...])`), and a pose (`volume=dict(pose_deg=...)`).

**`qa3d`** (classes: body, vial0, vial1, vial2, vial3, vial4, sphere, wedge, slot; without signal: plastic). A cylinder of gel with flat ends. About the isocentre lies the vial section: tubes along the axis, laid out exactly as the 2D `qa` phantom of the same seed and spec (section 5). Beyond it on one side: a ball of its own material and a pair of wedges whose tilted faces cross (in an axial slice at a height d above their centre the two bars differ in length by 2 d / tan(angle)). On the other side: a block of plastic with two thin crossed ramps of fluid (in an axial slice of thickness T a ramp shows as a bar of length T / slope, and the two bars move apart with the slice position). Which side is which is drawn. Options: the 2D `qa` spec (oil vials, an elongated body, placed discs), `volume=dict(features=False)` for the vial section alone, placed inserts, a pose.

### Regions and truth

`world["regions"]` lists 3D regions: balls, and cylinders along an axis (`shape`, centre `y_mm, x_mm, z_mm`, `radius_mm`, for a cylinder `axis` and `half_length_mm`). A region is listed only if its class fills at least 0.95 of it, measured on sample points of the recipe (`purity`). `world.truth(world)` returns the tissue values per region as for a 2D world; `world["volume"].truth()` adds the susceptibility. `world["layout"]` holds the geometry: positions are patient coordinates (the warp of the head is inverted for them), sizes are those of the recipe. For this seed `head3d` lists 18 regions and `qa3d` 7.

### Susceptibility

Volume susceptibility relative to soft tissue, ppm (SI), per class (`volume.susceptibility_ppm`); classes not listed are 0. Air (9.4) is the engine's own constant (`world.DCHI_AIR`). **Every other value is declared**: chosen by the authors of this package, of the size the literature reports, with no source opened for this module.

## 3. What is schematic

- This is drawn anatomy, not a segmented subject. There is no cerebellum, no brainstem, no cord, no falx or tentorium; the skull base is a shorter half of the same ellipsoid; the face and the neck are one muscle class; the neck is a cylinder that never widens into shoulders.
- Vessels are straight tubes. They carry a direction and a signed speed as numbers; nothing flows until a slice engine uses them.
- Sulci are sheets of a random field, not gyri with a name; the cortex has one thickness per head.
- The eyes are balls of the CSF class. Air cavities are ellipsoids.
- The test object is not a product: the vial section follows this package's 2D `qa` phantom, the ramps and wedges follow the idea of the ACR phantom (ramps of 1 in 10, crossed wedges) with drawn angles. The ACR dimensions belong to a later world (`acr3d`).
- A lesion is one class with one susceptibility; a vessel has the susceptibility declared for venous blood whichever way it runs.

## 4. How a plane is evaluated

`eval_points(y, x, z)` evaluates the recipe directly, in float64: every shape at every point. It is the reference and the verifier's truth. `eval_plane(frame, n, extent_mm)` evaluates the same recipe on the engine's grid in a plane with two shortcuts that change no value beyond rounding:

- a small shape is evaluated only inside the rectangle that its bounding box (plus the reach of the warp and of the soft edge) can touch on the plane;
- a smooth random field is a sum of plane cosines, and on a plane cos(a + b v + c u) = Re(e^{ia} e^{ibv} e^{icu}): one small matrix product instead of a cosine per mode and point. Nothing is interpolated.

The head is evaluated in float32 on a plane (in strips of rows that stay in the cache), the test object in float64. A plane at any position, angle and thickness offset is therefore the object itself at those points; partial volume across a slice is the slice engine's sum over several such planes. `lattice(spacing_mm)` evaluates axial planes on a coarse lattice (values at the voxel centres; `supersample=k` averages k^3 samples per voxel), for the field of the object and for 3D acquisitions.

## 6. Not verified

- No image exists of a volume world: nothing here was compared with a scan, and no number of this page is a property of an image.
- The anatomy was looked at on label maps of a few seeds; its realism is not measured.
- Times were taken on one shared machine; the other numbers on one numerical stack. On another NumPy build the bit-for-bit statements hold only as far as that build rounds elementary functions the same way.
- The susceptibilities other than air are declared, and nobody has signed them.
