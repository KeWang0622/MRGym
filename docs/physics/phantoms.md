## 1. ACR large phantom (world kind `acr`)

Source: *Large and Medium Phantom Test Guidance for the ACR MRI Accreditation Program* (American College of Radiology, the version dated 10.19.2022), read in full. A world is one section of the phantom on one hidden system: `acr=dict(slice=...)` with `slice` in 1, 5, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, sagittal.

### Declared by this package (the guidance does not give them)

- The solution (10 mM NiCl2, 75 mM NaCl, guidance 0.2) has T1 150 ms and T2 130 ms at both fields: constants chosen by the authors, the same for every seed. They set the signal ratio of the T1 and T2 series, nothing else.
- Layout inside a section: the block of the resolution insert (y 8 to 42 mm, x -48 to 48 mm), the stagger of the hole rows (0.25 x the diameter per row), the grid of slice 5 (pitch 15 mm, bars 1.2 mm), the place of the disks on a spoke (16, 28, 40 mm from the centre), the first spoke 9 degrees clockwise of 12 o'clock, and the eight disk diameters between the two the guidance states (a geometric progression: 7.00, 5.90, 4.97, 4.19, 3.53, 2.97, 2.51, 2.11, 1.78, 1.50 mm).
- The bandwidth of the series (130 Hz per pixel) and the healthy system: receiver noise density 0.0065 (3 T; twice that at 1.5 T), transmit gain within 3 %, gradients within 0.15 %. The noise level is a calibration, not a measurement: it was chosen so that a healthy system scores above the guidance's low-contrast limits at both fields.
- Two readers are replaced by stated rules. Resolution: a row of four holes is resolved when every gap is darker than both of its holes by 0.5 of the brightest hole. Low contrast: a disk is seen when its mean exceeds the mean of the ring around it by 4 standard errors.
- The artifact assessment of the guidance (section 8) is a reader's judgement with no number; here it is a detector of a line of interference in the background with the limit z <= 15.
- A plastic part displaces solution, so the low-contrast sheet lowers the weight of the solution by the contrast and its holes restore it. The contrast therefore does not change with the slice thickness of the protocol as it does in the phantom.

### Not simulated

- **slice thickness accuracy**: the two signal ramps are slots inclined through the slice ([G] 3.2); the world is one 2D section.
- **slice position accuracy**: the crossed 45 degree wedges are structure through the slice ([G] 4.2); the world is one 2D section.
- The localiser plane shows the outline of the phantom (a rectangle) and none of its inner structures.
- The series is acquired one section per scan; the console time of one scan is compared with the guidance's time of the 11-slice series (slices of a conventional spin echo are interleaved inside one TR). There is no multi-slice acquisition and no cross-talk.
- A gradient calibration error is a scaling of the encoded positions (`grad_scale` of the hidden system). The image intensity is not divided by the scale as the k-space scaling would.

Every row is the row `mrgym.qa.FAULTS` expects: True. The seven mechanisms of the matrix test: signal_instability, rf_interference, transmit_gain, shim, gradient_x, receiver_noise, b1_nonuniformity.

Three rows are findings rather than confirmations:

- **A centre-frequency error alone fails nothing.** The image moves by offset / bandwidth pixels and every test is relative to the phantom.
- **A rocking phantom fails resolution and low contrast, not the ghosting ratio.** It blurs the image along the motion and puts its ghost on the edges of the phantom; the four background regions of the ghosting test hardly see it at this amplitude. The guidance names motion as a cause of ghosting and ghosting as a cause of resolution and low-contrast failures.
- **A transmit fault fails low contrast as well as uniformity.** A wrong flip angle costs signal; for the local transmit fault the stated reader rule also loses the lowest-contrast disks on the slope of the intensity, where an eye would not.

## 4. NIST T1 plate (world kind `nist_t1`)

The NiCl2 array of the ISMRM / NIST system phantom as a 2D world. The object is the same for every seed; its position in the scanner (within 1 mm and 0.3 degrees, as for the ACR phantom) and the system round it are drawn.

Opened and used:

- Stupic et al. 2021 (article body): a spherical shell of 200 mm inner diameter; array spheres of 15 mm inner and 20 mm outer diameter; 10 spheres on a 50 mm radius and 4 inside on a 40 mm grid; the Ni-12 solution at 3 T and 20.0 degrees C: T1 44.53 ms, T2 31.86 ms; regions of 10 mm diameter.
- The reference T1 list of the ISMRM reproducibility challenge (`rrsg2020/analysis`, `src/nist.py`, phantoms with serial number below 0043, 20 degrees C): 1989, 1454, 984.1, 706, 496, 351.5, 247.13, 175.3, 125.9, 89, 62.7, 44.53, 30.84, 21.719 ms. Its twelfth value is the Ni-12 value of the article, which is how the list is tied to 3 T.

Not opened, and what follows from it:

- Tables S1 to S3 of the article (sphere positions, the 1.5 T values, T2 of every sphere). The world therefore exists at 3 T only; the order of the spheres on the plate (1 to 10 round the ring, 11 to 14 on the inner grid) is a choice; and T2 of a sphere is T1 x (31.86 / 44.53), the ratio of sphere 12, a declared rule and not the published T2.
- The relaxation times of the water round the spheres ("long-T1 deionized water" in the article): declared, T1 2800 ms, T2 2000 ms.
- The second list of the same file (serial numbers from 0043) was opened and is not used; the temperature correction of the file was not read.
- No published protocol has been run on this world yet (the inversion-recovery and variable-flip-angle protocols of the article and of the challenge): it is an object with reference values, not a validation result.
